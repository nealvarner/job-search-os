# Outreach Templates

10 scenarios. Fill brackets. Send from phone-friendly length. Specificity beats flattery.

**General rules.** Phone-readable. Never attach a resume on first contact — link to LinkedIn. Don't say "I'd love to pick your brain" — say "15-minute call." Pre-disclose any eventual ask. The 6-point email and 3B7 follow-up are the spine of cold outreach.

---

## 1. LinkedIn connection — hiring manager at target company

> Hi [Name] — saw you're hiring a [Role] on the [Team] at [Company]. I just applied. Your [recent post / shipped change / press release quote] on [specific topic] is part of why [Company] is on my shortlist. Not asking for anything — would love to be connected as I learn more about your team.

*Why:* Names the actual artifact. Pre-discloses the application. Explicit no-ask.

---

## 2. LinkedIn connection — peer / fellow alum / same function

> Hi [Name] — fellow [school / city / function / company alum] here. Saw your work on [specific thing]. I'm a [your role] thinking through my next move and your background lines up scarily well. Would love to connect.

*Why:* Lead with the trust transfer (alum, function, geography). Concrete observation about their work. Soft ask.

---

## 3. Cold email — informational interview (Dalton 6-point)

**Subject:** Fellow [alum / function / city] — quick question

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm a [one-line who you are — e.g. "TA leader at Reckitt, formerly Amazon HR"] exploring my next move in [target function/role], ideally with a company that has [specific kind of work].
>
> Your work on [specific project / launch / post] caught my eye — especially [one concrete detail, ~10 words].
>
> Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next couple weeks? I'd love to ask about trends you're seeing in [their field/company/region] and what advice you'd give someone making this move.
>
> Either way — thanks for the work you put out.
>
> [Your first name]
> [LinkedIn link]

*The 6 points:* credibility, identifier, commonality, request, end-time (15 min), thanks. Under 100 words.

## 4. Warm intro request (forwardable)

> Hey [Friend],
>
> Hope you're well. Short ask: would you intro me to [Person] at [Company]? Saw you're connected.
>
> I'm exploring [Role] roles and [Person]'s work on [specific thing] is exactly the conversation I'd love to have. **Forwardable blurb below so you can just hit forward:**
>
> ---
> *[Person] — meet [Your Name]. [One-line who they are, with the headline metric]. [Geo + open-to status]. They'd love 15 min on [specific topic]. LinkedIn: [link]. PDF: [link].*
> ---
>
> Totally fine to say no — I know intros cost reputation.

*Why:* Does 90% of the work for your friend. The forwardable blurb is the move 95% of askers skip.

---

## 5. The 50-person network blast (highest-ROI message in any search)

Send individually, not BCC. Personalize line 1 for each. Spend 30 sec per person.

**Subject:** A small favor (and an offer)

> Hey [Name],
>
> [One personal line — "saw your move to [city], congrats" / "still thinking about that conversation we had about [X]" / "hope your spring is treating you well"].
>
> Quick news: I'm starting a job search and figured you'd be one of the smartest people to loop in. Snapshot for easy forwarding:
>
> - **Looking for:** [Role title]
> - **Ideal companies:** [3-5 named companies]
> - **Strengths:** [2 bullets, quantified]
> - **Location:** [City / open to remote / open to relocate]
> - **Timing:** Looking to land by [month]
>
> **What would help most:**
> 1. Intros to anyone at the companies above (or similar)
> 2. A heads-up on roles you hear about before they're posted
> 3. Honest feedback if my [LinkedIn link / Me-on-a-Page link] is missing anything
>
> If you're job-hunting too, hit reply — happy to do the same for you.
>
> Thanks,
> [Name]

*Why:* Named companies + named roles lets people pattern-match instantly. The "what would help" menu beats "let me know if you hear anything" by 10x. Reciprocity offer removes the transactional feel.

## 6. Application follow-up (Day 7-10, no response)

**Subject:** Re: [Original thread subject — never start a new thread]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Following up on my application for [Role] from [date] in case it got buried. Still very interested — especially after [recent company news].
>
> One thing I didn't include in the application: [one new proof point — recent project, article, cert in progress].
>
> Happy to share more or step aside if the role's moved on. Thanks for considering.

**Cadence: Day 0 (apply) → Day 7 (this) → Day 14 (one more, very short) → stop.**

---

## 7. Post-interview thank-you (within 24 hrs, same day if possible)

**Subject:** Thanks — and a thought on [topic]

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the time today. Really enjoyed digging into [specific topic from convo].
>
> One thing that stuck with me: you mentioned [their challenge]. After the call I [sketched / thought through / drafted] [specific thing]. Happy to share if useful.
>
> Excited about [Company] and the [Team]. Let me know if there's anything else I can send over.

*Why:* Subject promises a *thought*, not just thanks — gets opened. "Thing I did after the call" beats every other line because it shows you're already operating on their problem.

## 8. Staying warm (2-4 weeks after a chat)

**Subject:** Thought of you when I saw this

> Hi [Name],
>
> Came across [article / launch / data point] on [topic you discussed] and immediately thought of our convo about [specific thing]. Link: [URL].
>
> No reply needed — just paying you back for the time you gave me.
>
> Hope [specific thing they mentioned] is going well.

*Why:* Give-before-ask. Inverts the usual "I only surface when I need something."

---

## 9. The referral ask (24-48 hours after a good informational interview)

**Subject:** Quick follow-up from yesterday

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks again for yesterday — your point about [specific insight] is going to change how I [do X].
>
> Wanted to ask directly: based on what we discussed, would you feel comfortable forwarding my resume to [hiring manager] for the [Role]? Totally understand if it's too early or not a fit — I'd rather you say no than feel awkward.
>
> If yes, I can send a 3-bullet blurb on my fit so it's effortless on your end.

*Why:* Names the awkwardness, gives a clean "no," makes "yes" effortless.

---

## 10. Inbound recruiter — keep the door open

> Hi [Name] — thanks for reaching out, appreciate you thinking of me.
>
> What would move me right now is something at the **[seniority level]** in **[function]**, ideally **[industry filter]**, **[geo preference]**.
>
> If something fitting comes up, I'd genuinely want to know. Happy to stay loosely in touch.

*Why:* Tells the recruiter exactly when to come back. Gift to them. Doesn't burn the bridge.

---

## 11. TIARA — the informational interview script

Ask in this order. The shift is intentional: start treating them as expert, end treating them as mentor.

- **T**rends: *"What trends in [their field] are you watching most closely right now?"*
- **I**nsights: *"What do you see that most people in our field miss?"*
- **A**dvice: *"For someone with my background trying to move into [target], what experiences should I prioritize next?"*
- **R**esources: *"Are there 1-2 other people in your network who'd be great for me to talk to?"* ← resources = people, not articles. This is the referral seed.
- **A**ssignments: *"What kinds of projects do strong people in [target role] tend to own in their first year?"*

Never ask for a job on the call. Send the referral ask (§9) 24-48 hours later.

## Subject lines that get opened

- `Fellow [alum / function / city] — quick question`
- `Thought of you when I saw this`
- `A small favor (and an offer)`
- `Quick follow-up from yesterday`
- `[Mutual contact] suggested I reach out`
- `Re: [original thread]` ← always for follow-ups, never start a new thread
- `One question about [their specific project]`

**Avoid:** "Touching base," "Circling back," "Opportunity," anything with "!"

---

## 5 hard rules — what NOT to do

1. **No "I'd love to pick your brain."** Dead phrase. Say "15-minute call" + specific topic.
2. **Never attach a resume on first contact.** Link LinkedIn.
3. **Don't open with "I know you're busy."** Wastes your first line.
4. **No life stories.** If it doesn't fit on a phone screen without scrolling, cut it.
5. **Don't follow up >3x without new info.** Each follow-up adds an artifact or doesn't get sent.

---

## The standout move — "do the work first"

For top 5-10 dream companies: spend ~90 min building a 1-3 page deliverable that solves a real problem they have. Send the link with:

> *"Made this for fun. No reply needed — just wanted you to have it."*

Response rates jump from ~5% to 30-50%. It proves you can do the job, triggers reciprocity, and is memorable. Single highest-leverage move in the entire search.
